## 1.1 Project Context

### 1.1.1 Organization Background
The Social Development Unit (SDU) is a primary institutional unit of Ateneo de Zamboanga University (AdZU). It is a core unit dedicated to realizing the university's Ignatian mission of forming men and women for others. Functioning as the central gateway for social advocacy, community engagement, and nation-building initiatives, the SDU promotes critical themes such as environmental governance, interfaith relations, cultural understanding, and peace. Through these efforts, the SDU effectively translates the university's commitment to academic excellence and spiritual values into concrete, practical social action.

The unit's mandate extends far beyond the university campus, aiming to foster peace and sustainable social development throughout Zamboanga City and the broader Mindanao region. The SDU holds different key programs and services that aim for improvement of social aspects, one such service is to actively partner with marginalized sectors, displaced communities, and Indigenous Peoples to deliver holistic, accessible, and transformative programs. Its organizational focus spans a wide spectrum of societal interventions ranging from building economic resilience and reducing poverty in underserved areas, to promoting inclusive, culturally sensitive learning environments that uphold human dignity and active civic participation.

To address these diverse and complex societal needs, the SDU operates through a collaborative network of specialized centers. Each center focuses on specific advocacy areas: such as cultural heritage preservation, environmental stewardship, health and livelihood empowerment, and leadership governance ensuring that the university's outreach is both targeted and highly impactful. Executing these multifaceted community extensions requires a highly competent, adaptable, and continuously trained workforce, driving the organization's heavy emphasis on regular staff capacity building.

Figure 1.1.5 illustrates the current cross-functional workflow, which spans three organizational layers: the Unit Director, the Office Heads, and the Staff. The process begins at the start of the academic year, when the Unit Director prepares and transmits the Audit Form (in the form of a shared Google Sheet) along with an audit directive and the corresponding link to the Office Heads. Upon receiving the audit form, each Office Head shares the tool with their respective staff and instructs them to begin encoding their information. Staff members then access the shared Google Sheet and manually input their personal and job-related details, namely their name, position, assigned program, degree attained, and the competencies needed for their job or position, before saving and exiting the sheet. As staff continue encoding their records, the Office Head monitors the sheet entries to check whether all assigned staff records are complete; if any records remain incomplete, the Office Head sends follow-up reminders to the concerned staff and continues monitoring until all entries are finished. Once the Office Head confirms that all assigned staff records are complete, they report their center's completion status to the Unit Director. On the Unit Director's end, confirmations are received from all six centers, after which the Unit Director manually processes and filters the consolidated spreadsheet data, attempts to identify competency gaps and track completions, and manually drafts the capacity summaries before the process ends. While this manual, decentralized approach allows for basic record-keeping, it places a heavy burden on the Unit Director, who must consolidate and analyze data from all six centers by hand, making the process time-consuming, error-prone, and difficult to scale.

While the personnel audit process described above is the organization's only established workflow, interviews with the Unit Director and APC Program Coordinator identified three additional areas of staff capacity management that are currently handled through informal or manual processes.

First, when the SDU receives requests for a resource speaker, moderator, or facilitator, the Unit Director currently identifies potential staff members by asking them individually about their availability and relevant experience. According to the APC Program Coordinator, this process is done manually because there is no existing system that provides a list of staff and their respective competencies (APC Program Coordinator, personal communication, July 27, 2026). Staff may therefore be identified through word-of-mouth or by referring to information in their resumes. In practice, requests are often directed to offices such as ACLG and APC because their staff members are already known to have experience handling similar activities.

Second, staff development funds are currently managed at the office level. Each office has its own allocated budget for staff development, while requests and budget monitoring are handled through the Purchasing Custodian Office (PCO) system, which is integrated with the university's finance system. The APC Program Coordinator explained that offices can submit requests through the PCO system and check their available budget through the same system (APC Program Coordinator, personal communication, July 27, 2026). Funds received from partner organizations are managed separately from the budget provided by the university. Any remaining funds are returned rather than carried over to the following year (APC Program Coordinator, personal communication, July 27, 2026).

Lastly, records related to staff activities and office operations are currently maintained through a shared Google Drive. According to the APC Program Coordinator, documents such as training certificates, reports, MOUs/MOAs, and proposals are uploaded and organized in the shared drive, which is also manually updated for documentation and accreditation purposes (APC Program Coordinator, personal communication, July 27, 2026). Training certificates are also kept as proof of staff participation in capacity-building activities.

The primary motivation for this project is to support the SDU's digital transformation. By moving from a manual spreadsheet to a centralized, automated platform, the organization can ensure better data integrity and more efficient monitoring. This upgrade allows the Unit Director to focus on strategic planning rather than manual data processing.

## 1.2 Statement of the Problem and Purpose
Through interviews with the Unit Director and APC Program Coordinator, it was found that the Social Development Unit (SDU) has no dedicated system for managing staff information and competency development. Instead, staff information is recorded through Google Sheets, while training certificates and related documents are stored in Google Drive. This manual approach presents challenges in organizing and monitoring staff capacity-building activities across the six centers. Several issues were identified:

Inefficient data input. As illustrated in Figure 1.2.1, shows the current Personnel Audit Form for the Staffs across SDU. In the interview, the primary concern of the program coordinator is the manual inputting of data at the start of the academic year. Staff are required to manually input essential information including names, positions, assigned programs, degrees attained, and required competencies for their job/position into a shared Google Sheet. The manual process of inputting staff data into spreadsheets is labor-intensive. It takes time to manually enter staff data into a spreadsheet, particularly for larger teams per offices. Staff might have to spend a lot of time completing the form, time that would be better spent on their main responsibilities.

To address this problem, the proposed SDU Integrated Staff Capacity Building Management System provides a centralized digital platform with standardized forms for recording staff profile and training information. This reduces repetitive manual encoding and allows staff data to be stored and managed in one organized system instead of repeatedly entering information.

High Risk of Data Inconsistency and Human Error. Another problem in the current process is that staff members are manually typing their information into a shared Google Sheet, leaving the data very prone to human error. It is common to find simple misspellings, duplicate names, or even different ways of writing the same degree or position. For example, one person might type 'Bachelor of Science' while another types 'BS,' making it hard to sort the list accurately. These small errors add up and undermine the overall credibility of the audit. When the data is inconsistent, it becomes a huge challenge for the Unit Director to get a clear picture of what the staff is actually capable of. If the records are inaccurate, it is nearly impossible to figure out who has the right competencies or where more training is actually needed. In the end, if the foundation of the data is flawed, any plans made for staff development might be based on wrong information.

To address this problem, the proposed SDU Integrated Staff Capacity Building Management System uses standardized data fields and form validation to promote consistent data entry. By storing information in a centralized database, the system reduces duplicate records, inconsistent formatting, and other common errors.

Structural limitations in data management and real-time reporting. Another problem in the current process is that a spreadsheet-based format lacks the relational database architecture required for refined institutional analytics. Consequently, the SDU lacks a living data environment, resulting in several critical operational gaps:
• Absence of Automated Analytics and Visualization: The Google Sheet format does not support native, automated features for advanced filtering, trend analysis, or dashboard reporting. Under the current process, data remains static. The Unit Director cannot readily generate high-level summaries or visualize the distribution of staff competencies across different programs. The absence of automated reporting hinders the SDU's ability to conduct longitudinal tracking. Consequently, the unit cannot compare current staff qualifications with historical data, which is essential for measuring institutional growth.
• Inefficiency in Decision-Support Systems: The current system also makes it difficult for the Unit Director to actually use the data for decision-making. If the Unit Director needs to see how many staff members have finished their training or where the team is lacking specific skills, they have to manually sort through rows of data and calculate the numbers themselves. This manual data processing is not only a waste of time but also leaves a lot of room for mistakes. Every time the Unit Director has to manually re-sort or copy data for a report, there is a risk of miscalculating the results, which could lead to the wrong decisions being made about staff training and development.
• Lack of Proactive Monitoring: The current system does not have a way to alert anyone when something is wrong. Since Google Sheets does not send out automatic notifications or reminders, the whole process of keeping track of staff qualifications is reactive. This means problems such as staff members missing a required competency are usually not noticed until someone manually checks the sheet or, worse, until a problem actually happens during a program audit. Without a system that can automatically flag these gaps, important qualifications might be overlooked, which could eventually affect the unit's accreditation or its day-to-day operations.

To address these problems, the proposed SDU Integrated Staff Capacity Building Management System provides a central monitoring dashboard, filtering tools, and data export and printing capabilities. These features allow the unit director and secretary to access consolidated staff and training data, monitor progress across offices, and generate reports.

Absence of a director-level capacity tracker for strategic decision-making. Another problem in the current process is that through a follow-up interview with the Unit Director, it became apparent that while the Human Resources office of the university maintains general staff records, the SDU lacks its own director-level system to track the "big picture" of staff competency in a way that directly supports operational decision-making. Three specific decision-support gaps have been identified:
• No Visibility into Participation Roles: The current system records only whether a staff member attended a training, but not the capacity in which they participated. The Director cannot currently distinguish whether a staff member attended as a Facilitator, Participant, Speaker, or Organizer, a distinction that is critical for identifying true staff expertise. For example, if the SDU needs to organize an in-house training on a specific topic, the Director must rely on memory to identify which staff member has served as a Speaker or Facilitator in that area. The absence of participation role data prevents the SDU from accurately mapping and utilizing its internal expertise.
• No Mechanism to Detect Over-Participation or Unequal Training Distribution: The current system provides no way to detect when a particular staff member is attending training disproportionately often, such as every month over a six-month period. Without this visibility, the Unit Director cannot intervene to redirect training opportunities to other staff members who may have had no development activities in the same period. This results in an unequal distribution of capacity-building opportunities across the unit.
• No Unit-Wide Competency Gap Identification: The current system cannot identify skill deficits at the organizational level. For instance, if none of the SDU's staff across all six centers have received training in Community Organizing or Project Management, the Director has no automated way to surface this gap. This forces the SDU to remain in a reactive, invitation-led mode waiting for external training invitations to arrive rather than proactively identifying gaps and organizing targeted in-house training. A system capable of mapping required competencies against actual training records would allow the SDU to shift toward a more strategic, planning-led approach to staff development.

To address these problems, the proposed SDU Integrated Staff Capacity Building Management System provides a central monitoring dashboard that gives the unit director and secretary a consolidated view of staff training records and capacity information across all SDU offices. The system records participation roles and uses training gap analysis to identify competency gaps and unequal training participation. These features allow the unit director or secretary to make informed decisions regarding staff development, training distribution, and the use of available expertise.

Absence of a mechanism for matching staff competencies to external requests. Another problem in the current process is that there is no systematic way to match staff skills with incoming requests for technical assistance or resource-person engagements. For instance, when an external partner such as UN Women requests personnel with a specific set of skills, the Unit Director does not have a centralized system to search and compare staff qualifications, and instead relies on personal knowledge or past experience. While this approach may work for a smaller number of requests, it can become less efficient and reliable as the unit grows. Without a proper system for competency matching, there is also a possibility that qualified staff may be overlooked while the same individuals are repeatedly selected for external engagements.

This has created a pattern where external requests are often directed to offices that are already known to handle them, particularly ACLG and APC. According to the APC Program Coordinator, these requests are commonly assigned to the two offices because there is limited information available about the competencies of staff from the other centers. However, the number of staff in ACLG and APC is limited, making it difficult for them to accommodate every request. Redirecting the requests to other offices is also challenging because there is no centralized way to determine whether their staff have the knowledge or skills needed for a particular activity (APC Program Coordinator, personal communication, July 27, 2026). This does not necessarily mean that ACLG and APC have more qualified staff than the other centers. Rather, their competencies are more familiar to the Unit Director because they have previously handled similar requests. As a result, the same staff members may repeatedly receive external assignments, which can contribute to an uneven distribution of workload. The Program Coordinator also expressed this concern during the interview, noting that repeatedly assigning requests to the same offices can become tiring for the staff involved (APC Program Coordinator, personal communication, July 27, 2026). On the other hand, staff from other centers who may have similar or even stronger qualifications can be overlooked because their skills and experiences are not formally documented in a centralized system. At present, the SDU may rely on individual staff resumes when identifying qualifications. However, these resumes generally reflect the skills and experiences of staff at the time they were hired and may not be updated as they gain new competencies through training, facilitation, or fieldwork. Without a centralized and regularly updated skills inventory, staff matching may therefore rely on familiar assignments and outdated hiring records rather than current information about staff competencies. This makes it more difficult to distribute external opportunities fairly and to maximize the skills and expertise available across the SDU.

To address this problem, the proposed SDU Integrated Staff Capacity Building Management System includes a skills tracking and matching module that maintains a searchable profile of each staff member's skill and competencies. The unit director and secretary can search and filter staff based on specific skills, making it easier to identify qualified personnel for external requests. This helps distribute opportunities more fairly across the different SDU centers.

Absence of a mechanism for measuring actual capacity growth. Another problem in the current process is that there is no system for determining whether staff training results in real improvement in skills or work performance. Just because staff attended a training does not automatically mean capacity building actually happened. Based on Mulchandani (2026), organizations usually measure capacity building through a combination of things, not just attendance. This includes comparing staff's skills and knowledge before and after a training to see if actual learning took place, checking if there are improvements in how work gets done such as project delivery, service quality, or resource management, and looking at whether systems like governance, leadership, and decision-making have gotten stronger over time. It also involves gathering feedback from the people involved, like staff, partners, or the communities being served, to see the real impact of the training, and checking long-term outcomes such as whether the organization has become more resilient and better prepared to carry out its mission. This is relevant to SDU-ISCMS because the current process can only show that a staff member showed up to a training. It does not show whether they actually learned anything from it, applied it, or still remember it after. Based on the literature, this combination of measures is what real capacity building evaluation is supposed to look at, not just counting who attended what.

This problem is not simply a result of the current system or the expansion of the SDU into six centers. Instead, it reflects a long-standing gap in how staff development has been monitored. This was also supported by the APC Program Coordinator, who explained that the unit's reporting practices have traditionally focused on keeping proof of participation, such as certificates and attendance records. Staff members are generally advised to keep their training certificates for future purposes, such as rank and promotion applications. The Program Coordinator shared that she keeps her certificates in a folder and believes that other staff members in the unit do the same (APC Program Coordinator, personal communication, July 27, 2026). The Program Coordinator also pointed out that when these documents are lost or unavailable, it becomes difficult to determine which training activities a staff member has attended, since physical certificates and e-certificates are the main forms of verification. This shows that the current process mainly focuses on documenting whether staff participated in training rather than determining how these activities contributed to the development of their skills, competencies, or work performance.

To address this problem, the proposed SDU Integrated Staff Capacity Building Management System includes an evaluation module that allows the Unit Director to evaluate the Office Head, and the Office Head to assess a Staff member's demonstrated proficiency in a specific skill after completing a training. Using a rating scale from 1 to 5, the system records and monitors changes in individual competency over time, allowing the SDU to track staff members' skill improvement through capacity building activities.

Absence of structured tracking for partner contributions and budget utilization. Another problem in the current process is that a significant portion of the unit's programs are co-funded or fully funded by external partners, but the individual centers do not have a consolidated way to track which activities are supported by each funding source, how much each partner contributes, or how much allocated funds have already been used across the six centers. This makes it difficult for the Unit Director to provide accurate financial reports to partner organizations, compare the costs of activities across centers, and plan future programs based on the status of existing partnerships. There is also no automated alert system that can notify staff when a budget allocation is close to being fully used. As a result, overspending or depleted funds may only be noticed when an office needs to use the remaining budget for an activity that is already on-going. Without a structured budget tracking mechanism, the SDU still relies on manually recording and checking after they have been made. This makes it difficult for the unit to monitor available funds in real time and make timely decisions about program implementation, funding needs, and external partnerships.

To address this problem, the proposed SDU Integrated Staff Capacity Building Management System includes budget management and partner institution management modules. The budget management module allows unit director and secretary to monitor allocated, spent, and remaining funds, record itemized expenses, identify funding resources, and receive alerts when a budget is nearing depletion. Meanwhile, the partner institution management module maintains a centralized record of partner organizations, agreements, and their contribution to SDU activities. These features provide the SDU with a more organized view of its financial resources and external partnerships for monitoring and planning purposes.

If the user chooses to log partner funding, the system explicitly verifies whether the user is a Unit Director or Secretary: if validated, the user inputs partner contribution details, and the system saves the funding record to the Budget DB before returning to M.1; if unvalidated, it exits directly to M.1. In contrast to administrative allocations, expense logging has no role-restriction check, allowing any staff member to log a training expense. The user inputs expense details along with a supporting receipt file, and the system saves both the expense record and receipt file directly to the Budget DB. The system then evaluates if total expenses reach or exceed 80% of the budget: if true, the system sends an overspend alert to the Unit Director, Secretary, and Office Head, logs the alert in the Notifications DB, displays a success message, and exits to M1; if false, it displays a success message and exits directly to M1.

Finally, if the user chooses to export a budget report, they select report criteria, allowing the system to query the Budget DB to generate, display, and export the budget report before returning to M.1. Selecting an invalid action or completing any process path systematically terminates back at the M.1 off-page connector, completing the module cycle.

## 1.3 Objectives

### 1.3.1 General objective:
The general objective of this study is to develop a comprehensive digital system that enables the Social Development Unit (SDU) to efficiently manage, record, monitor, and evaluate staff training activities and competencies.

### 1.3.2 Specific Objectives:
The following are specific objectives that are included in the system:

1. To develop a Secure Account Management System with Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) that allows users to register, log in, and log out, enables the Unit Director or a delegated authority to approve accounts, and restricts access to data and functions based on assigned roles.
2. To develop a Training Input Module that allows Staff and Office Heads to add, view, edit, and delete training records, including the training title, description, dates, venue, nature, scope, category, required skills, and participation role (Facilitator, Participant, Speaker, or Organizer).
3. To develop a Training Assignment Module that enables the Unit Director and Office Heads to create, assign, edit, delete, filter, and monitor staff training assignments, including specifying the training title, description, deadline, categories, scope, venue, and required skills, while tracking training completion through a centralized Assignment Board.
4. To develop a Central Monitoring Dashboard for the Unit Director to view unit-wide training records, track staff compliance, and monitor progress across the unit, with interactive and filterable modules.
5. To develop an Office Head Oversight Function that allows Office Heads to view training activities, review submitted training records and supporting proofs, approve or reject submissions from staff within their respective offices, monitor participation roles, and assess office-specific capacity details through a centralized dashboard.
6. To develop a Staff Dashboard that allows Staff to view and edit their profiles, add, edit, delete, and view their training records and participation records, and view and manage assigned tasks in an organized and accessible dashboard.
7. To develop a Participation Role Classification feature that records and displays whether a staff member attended a training as a Facilitator, Participant, Speaker, or Organizer, enabling the Unit Director to accurately identify internal expertise and capacity.
8. To develop a Training Gap Analysis feature that flags unit-wide competency deficits and identifies staff members with disproportionately high or low training participation, supporting equitable and strategic training distribution.
9. To develop a Communication Module featuring an integrated Inbox and Notification System that enables Staff, Office Heads, and the Unit Director to send, receive, view, read, and reply to direct messages, while allowing the Unit Director and Office Heads to compose and broadcast system notifications to specific offices or user groups, with Staff having view-only access to incoming notifications.
10. To develop a Proof Upload Module that enables Staff and Office Heads to upload and submit multiple supporting document files or images for completed training activities, allows Office Heads to review, approve, or reject submissions from staff within their respective offices, and enables the Unit Director or Secretary to validate or reject submitted proofs.
11. To develop a Filter Module for organizing and querying staff and training data by office, role, participation type, and time period, including bi-yearly periods corresponding to the first and second half of the academic year.
12. To develop Data Export and Printing Module capabilities that allow records to be exported to CSV or printed in a structured format (Ateneo → SDU → Staff Directory and Training Report) for administrative purposes.
13. To develop a Budget Management that enables the delegated authority to add, edit, and view office budget records, add expenses with supporting receipts, view funding sources and budget summaries, receive automated low-fund alerts, and generate printable financial reports.
14. To develop a Partner Institution Management that enables the Unit Director or Secretary to manage partner institution records system-wide, and Office Heads to add and edit partner institution records associated with their own office, including the designation of each partnership's lead implementing office, upload MOA/MOU and related documents, and manage partner contribution details.
15. To develop a Skills Tracking and Matching Module that enables the Unit Director and Secretary to quickly identify staff based on their skills and competencies.
16. To develop an Evaluation Module that enables the Unit Director to rate Office Heads and Office Heads to rate Staff based on demonstrated skill proficiencies associated with completed training program using a 1-to-5 proficiency rating and optional comments.

## 1.4 Scope and Limitations

### 1.4.1 Scope of the Project
The proposed SDU Integrated Staff Capacity Building Management System is a web-based application designed to enable the Social Development Unit (SDU) to efficiently manage, record, monitor, and evaluate staff training activities and competencies. The system is accessible through web browsers and includes a mobile-responsive version for access through mobile devices. It is designed to encompass the SDU and its specialized centers, including ACCA, ACES, ACLG, ALTEC, APC, and CCES.

The intended users are categorized into four role-based access levels: the Unit Director, Secretary, Office Head, and Staff members. To fulfill its general objective, the system will cover the following specific functionalities and modules:

• Account Management: A secure system featuring Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) for user registration, login, and logout, requiring approval from the Unit Director or a delegated authority to ensure role-appropriate data access.
• Training Management: A Training Input Module for entering details such as title, venue, date, description, nature, category, scope, required skills, and participation role (Facilitator, Participant, Speaker, or Organizer), alongside a Training Assignment Module for the Unit Director and Office Heads to allocate tasks and track completion.
• Participation Role Classification: A dedicated feature for recording the specific role each staff member held in every training activity. Roles such as Facilitator, Participant, Speaker, and Organizer are selectable via checkboxes and are usable as data filters in dashboards and reports to support strategic capacity planning.
• Training Gap Analysis: A monitoring feature that identifies unit-wide competency deficits and flags staff members with disproportionately high or low training participation over a given period, enabling the Unit Director to make equitable and informed training decisions.
• File Validation: A Proof Upload Module where Staff and Office Heads submit multiple supporting document files or images for completed training activities. Office Heads can review, approve, or reject submissions made by staff within their respective offices. Submissions made by Office Heads are routed directly to the Unit Director or Secretary for validation or rejection. Rejected submissions include a stated reason and may be resubmitted by the original submitter.
• System Dashboards: An interactive Central Monitoring Dashboard for the Unit Director, an Office Head Oversight Function for managing specific centers, and a Staff Dashboard for individual profile and training record management. The Secretary is provided with full administrative delegation to assume Director-level access when the Unit Director is unavailable.
• Communication: Includes an integrated Inbox and Notification System enabling direct messaging among users and system broadcast notifications sent by the Unit Director or Office Heads to specific offices or user groups, with Staff having view-only access to incoming notifications.
• Data Filtering, Export, and Reporting: Features a Filter Module to query records by office, role, participation type, and bi-yearly periods (1st/2nd half of the academic year). Includes a Data Export and Printing Module to export datasets to CSV or print structured reports.
• Budget Management: The delegated authority has access to track finances through a three-level view (Office Totals, Funded Events, and Expense Breakdowns). It tracks allocated, spent, and remaining funds, differentiates funding sources, logs itemized expenses with supporting receipts, displays budget alerts (e.g., Over 80% spent), and generates printable expense reports.
• Partner Institution Management: Enables the Unit Director or Secretary to manage partner institution records system-wide, while Office Heads can add and edit partner records associated with their respective offices. The module includes the designation of the lead implementing office, contact details, MOA/MOU and related document uploads, agreement expiration alerts, and quarterly contribution breakdowns, including training counts, staff reached, and monetary or in-kind value.
• Skills Tracking and Matching: A module that allows the Unit Director or Secretary to filter and match personnel based on specific skills or competencies. It displays a dynamic list of matching staff members alongside their assigned SDU office unit and complete skill profile to help the Unit Director or Secretary in task assignment and capacity planning.
• Evaluation: A module that allows the Unit Director to evaluate an Office Head, and an Office Head to review their unit staff, select a training program the staff member completed, and assign a proficiency rating from 1 to 5 for a specific skill. It also includes a comment box for optional brief feedback.

During the initial scoping discussions, the possibility of expanding the system beyond the Social Development Unit (SDU) to other university offices, such as Admissions or Finance, was raised. However, the study was ultimately limited to the SDU instead of covering the entire university. This decision was based on the differences in the functions, workflows, and data requirements of each office. For example, the Registrar primarily handles student records, while the Finance Office focuses on financial transactions. These functions are different from the staff capacity management needs of the SDU. Expanding the system to accommodate all university offices would therefore require the system to cover a much wider range of functions, which could shift its purpose from addressing SDU's specific needs to becoming a general university-wide administrative or human resource system.

The researchers therefore decided to focus first on developing and validating the system within the SDU. This approach allows the system to directly address the unit's existing needs, particularly in monitoring staff skills, training, and improvements in staff competencies over time. Focusing on one unit also reduces the risk of creating a system that attempts to accommodate too many different functions without effectively addressing the specific needs of any one office.

The decision to keep the system SDU-specific was also supported by the results of an interview with the APC Program Coordinator conducted on July 27, 2026. According to the Program Coordinator, other university offices do not have the same need for a staff capacity management system because their functions and primary clients are different. Offices such as the Registrar primarily serve students and manage student-related concerns, while the SDU's programs are largely community-facing and involve activities and competencies that extend beyond the university. As explained by the Program Coordinator, this difference makes the SDU's capacity management needs distinct from those of other university offices (APC Program Coordinator, personal communication, July 27, 2026). When asked about the possibility of extending the system to other offices, the Program Coordinator also expressed that SDU preferred to keep the system focused on its own operations rather than extend it to other offices (APC Program Coordinator, personal communication, July 27, 2026). It further reflected the practical consideration that the system should remain focused on the needs and workflows of the SDU. Thus, the study prioritizes developing a system that is appropriate and useful for the SDU.

Based on these findings, the researchers decided to keep the system SDU-specific while expanding its functionality. This involved expanding the concept of capacity within the SDU by adding skills tracking and matching, partner institution management, budget tracking, and individual skills evaluation, along with training gap analysis, role-based access control, and more detailed reporting, allowing the project to grow in depth rather than organizational reach.

### 1.4.2 Limitations of the Project
To establish clear boundaries and constraints for the development phase, the proposed system is subject to the following limitations:

• Operational Boundary: The system's recording and monitoring functions are strictly limited to the training activities and competency profiles of the SDU staff. It will not cover or integrate with the staff capacity records of other independent departments or units within the university. This boundary reflects a deliberate scope decision made in consultation with the Unit Director and APC Program Coordinator (Section 1.4.1), rather than a default constraint of the system's design.
• Platform Limitation: The system is limited to a web-based platform and does not include the development of a separate mobile application.
• Functional Boundary: The system is focused on the management of staff capacity-building activities and related information within the SDU. It is not a comprehensive Human Resource Information System (HRIS) and will not handle financial payroll, daily time records, or general university recruitment processes.
• Financial Data Boundary: The Budget Management Module records office and partner funds and expenses based on information provided by users. It is not connected to the official financial records of the PCO or Finance Office. Therefore, the financial data in the system should be used for monitoring purposes only and not treated as the university's official financial record.
• Partner Institutions Limitation: The Partner Institution Management Module is limited exclusively to external organizations (such as government agencies, NGOs, and academic institutions). Internal programs, including staff formation, retreats, and recollections, are excluded from the system.
• Skills Data Reliability: Skills recorded in the system are based on staff self-reports and uploaded supporting documents. The system does not independently verify whether the skills or competencies reported by staff are accurate.
• Evaluation Scope: The Evaluation Module focuses only on individual staff members and evaluates specific skills in relation to completed training activities. It does not replace or integrate with the QA Office's existing office-level strategic tools, such as Target Performance Metrics (TPM), Operational Plan Metrics (OPM), Performance Evaluation Metrics (PEM), and Activity Implementation Monitoring Metrics (AIMM).
• Participation Role Classification Depth: Participation Role Classification records the category of a staff member's involvement in a training (Facilitator, Participant, Speaker, or Organizer) but does not distinguish the extent or substance of that involvement within the same category. For instance, a staff member who delivered brief opening remarks and one who served as the event's full facilitator would both be recorded identically as "Speaker" or "Facilitator." This limitation is offset by the Evaluation Module, where Office Head assigns a 1-5 competency rating per demonstrated skill, providing the depth that basic role classification lacks.
• Export Constraints: The generation of external data reports is limited strictly to CSV file exports and printed administrative formats.

## 1.5 Significance of the Project
The proposed SDU Integrated Staff Capacity Building Management System for the Social Development Unit (SDU) at Ateneo de Zamboanga University (AdZU) aims to improve the recording, monitoring, and evaluation of staff training activities and competencies within the SDU. This proposed system is beneficial to the following:

Unit Director, This proposed system is beneficial to the Unit Director, as they serve as the primary administrator responsible for overseeing all staff training activities and competency development across the unit. The system reduces the manual effort required to compile and verify training records by providing a Central Monitoring Dashboard that displays real-time compliance data, completion statuses, and progress across all offices. Beyond basic monitoring, the system's participation role classification and training gap analysis features give the Unit Director the strategic insight needed to make informed decisions about training distribution, internal capacity utilization, and targeted staff development, changing the SDU from a reactive to a proactive approach. As the Secretary holds Director-equivalent access within the system, this benefit extends to them as well, ensuring continuity of oversight and decision-making support even when the Unit Director is unavailable.

Office Head, This proposed system is beneficial to Office Head, as they are responsible for supervising the training compliance and capacity development of the staff under their respective offices. The system provides them with an oversight function that gives real-time visibility into the training activities, participation roles, and competency details of each staff member in their office, without having to rely on manually compiled reports from the Unit Director.

SDU Staff, SDU staff members benefit from having a dedicated Staff Dashboard that consolidates their training history, participation roles, assigned tasks, and profile information in one accessible location. The system allows them to input training details through a standardized form, upload proof of completed trainings, and receive notifications regarding assignments and deadlines directly within the platform. This promotes transparency and personal accountability in their own capacity development.

Social Development Unit, At the organizational level, the SDU benefits from a single, accessible digital database containing all staff training records from various offices and time periods. The system allows for faster and more reliable bi-yearly compliance reporting via filter and export functions, reduces the administrative workload associated with manual record-keeping, and provides management with the data needed to identify training gaps and strategically plan for the professional development of its workforce.

Future Researchers, this study serves as a useful reference for future researchers exploring training and capacity management systems in public sector, nonprofit, or institutional settings, particularly those involving multi-center organizations where strategic staff development is a core operational concern. It may also offer a useful starting point for researchers interested in expanding to other university offices. The system's role based structure of Unit Director, Secretary, Office Head, and Staff is general enough to support other organizational units; however, such expansion would require dedicated requirements-gathering with each office to define its distinct capacity-tracking processes, which falls outside the scope and timeline of this study.
